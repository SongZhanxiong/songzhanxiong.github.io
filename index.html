Population research for the decisions insurers, pension funds, and policymakers actually make.

I am a doctoral researcher at the Department of Demography, University of Vienna, supervised by Prof. Erich Striessnig. I work at the intersection of demography and actuarial science. My doctoral project asks how population structure — ageing, educational expansion, and shrinking households — shapes the harvesting effect in temperature-related mortality, what this means for educational inequalities in lifespan, and how reliable today’s actuarial mortality assumptions remain in a warming climate. I am also interested in how people perceive their own survival chances and how those beliefs shape retirement and annuity decisions.

Before turning to demography, I spent six years as a pricing actuary and actuarial consultant in Shanghai. I am an Associate of the Society of Actuaries (ASA) and have completed all FSA exams; the DMAC module is in progress.

Doctoral project · 2026 –

A Demographic Analysis of the Role of Population Structure in the Harvesting Effect

Zhanxiong Song · supervised by Prof. Erich Striessnig

PhD in Demography, Department of Demography, University of Vienna

Why it matters. Climate-adjusted, education-specific life tables that pension funds and insurers can use to stress-test the mortality assumptions behind long-term liabilities — and that show policymakers who bears the risk of a warming climate.

Extreme heat and cold both raise mortality, and a mortality spike can be followed by a deficit as the pool of frail individuals is depleted — the harvesting effect. The project treats population structure not as a background control but as a driver of the magnitude, direction, and social distribution of this mortality displacement.

Working papers

Subjective Survival Optimism, Retirement Timing, and Annuity Holding: Evidence from 19 European Countries

Zhanxiong Song, Cosmo Strozza, Paola Vázquez-Castillo

Working paper · Poster, European Population Conference 2026, Bologna · Data: SHARE

Why it matters. People who expect to live longer save, retire, and buy annuities differently. Understanding these beliefs helps insurers design retirement products and helps policymakers set retirement ages that people will actually act on.

Theses

Educational Inequalities in Life Expectancy and the Future of Pension Equity in Austria

Zhanxiong Song

Master’s thesis, Master of Global Demography, University of Vienna, 2025 · Supervisors: Sonja Spitzer & Miguel Sánchez-Romero

Why it matters. When life expectancy differs by education, a uniform retirement age quietly transfers pension wealth from the less to the more educated. The thesis quantifies this for Austria’s pension system.
